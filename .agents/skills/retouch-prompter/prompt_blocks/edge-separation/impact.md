## Edge Separation (Impact)

Two light sources illuminate the subject: the main frontal light and a secondary backlight positioned high and to the right at a 3/4 angle, creating rim light along the full silhouette. The rim light is strongest on the top of the head and the right side, and weaker on the left — it must not be uniform or symmetrical on both sides. Add a rim light along the subject's contour — a clear gray edge where the backlight catches the edges of the face, ears, hair, shoulders, and clothing. The rim light is prominently present and immediately noticeable — it separates the subject from the blue background and must not be faint or ambiguous. Apply rim lighting tracing the silhouette on the subject's surface (illuminating skin, fabric edges, hair strands). Vary rim light intensity — stronger on the illuminated side, weaker on the shadow side.
This applies to the silhouette edge only, not to facial features — facial transitions must remain gradual, no harsh black stripes on skin, work with light not with lines.
The rim light on clothing edges reads just above the adjacent fabric tone — readable on stone while remaining visually connected to the fabric. The rim light reads as bright gray, distinctly brighter than the adjacent skin or fabric, but never white.
