# Лазерная гравировка 80W (Laser 80W Style)

1. **Face & Skin (Professional Studio Retouch):**
   Render the skin as professional studio retouching — smooth and even in base tone, but with SUBTLE micro-texture and preserved major wrinkles that give the face character and help the laser create natural tonal variation on stone. Soften skin texture to a smooth, even base while preserving the PRESENCE of major wrinkles (forehead, nasolabial) as soft tonal transitions — do not erase them entirely, as they are essential for likeness and naturalism on stone. Avoid a "waxy" or "plastic" look by ensuring strong 3D volumetric depth through light and shadow. The stone itself provides the final texture; the file must be clean but not sterile — the face should look like a beautifully restored photograph, not an airbrushed illustration. Eliminate all individual pores, freckle-scale spots, and digital noise, but keep the structural wrinkles as soft tonal gradients.

2. **Hair Rendering (Dark Volume with Subtle Highlights):**
   Render hair as a dark, sculptural mass with SUBTLE highlight streaks that follow the hair's natural growth direction. Hair must remain noticeably DARKER than the face (at least 2-3 stops). Highlight streaks should be subtle and define volume — they must NOT dominate the portrait or make hair as bright as the face. The laser reads density differences; even subtle highlights on a dark base create readable texture on stone. Hair must have clear SCULPTURAL VOLUME — it must look like a 3D mass with a distinct beginning, curve, and end. Avoid flat, evenly-toned hair; maintain dark mass and soft highlight accents. Avoid fine individual strands; instead, create confident, sculptural locks with subtle light ridges.

3. **Materiality & Sculpted Volume:**
   Capture clothing with sculpted 3D volume through smooth, high-contrast shading. Clothing may contain lighter elements (collar, lace, buttons) but must never appear as a flat white blowout without detail. The key rule: clothing must have READABLE TEXTURE and tonal variation — a bright collar with visible detail is acceptable; a solid white shape with no detail is not. Render clothing with smooth tonal transitions that preserve fabric definition. Avoid imitating wool or grain; instead, convey the structure through smooth, high-contrast shading.

**Goal (Laser 80W Optimization):** Create a professional studio memorial portrait with natural, dignified, and warm materiality — the result must look like a beautifully restored photograph, not an airbrushed illustration. Target MEDIAN skin brightness 190-210 in the AI-generated grayscale file (before pipeline processing), with CLEAR tonal variation: highlight zones (forehead, cheekbones, nose bridge) must be NOTICEABLY BRIGHTER than shadow zones — highlights at ~200-220, shadows at ~140-170 (shadows visibly darker, roughly 2-3 stops below highlights). Do NOT render the entire face at a uniform high brightness — volume requires tonal range; the face must have a clear sense of light falling from one direction with bright catching surfaces and receding darker areas. The pipeline will lift the median to a final range of 210-230, preserving the tonal spread. Dark sculptural hair with subtle highlights, preserved facial character, clean volumetric depth without harsh graphic lines. No skin area may exceed brightness 235 except eye whites — this leaves headroom for the pipeline's adaptive brightness correction. Use an **f/11 aperture** setting for maximum depth of field and sharpness. Do NOT add bright rim light or halo — the pipeline adds edge glow programmatically.
