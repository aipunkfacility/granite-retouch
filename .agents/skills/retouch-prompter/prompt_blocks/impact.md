# Ударная гравировка (Impact Style)

1. **Face & Skin (Smooth Tonal Variation):**
   Render the skin SMOOTH with SUBTLE tonal micro-variation — NOT individual pores, NOT speckled texture, NOT mottled grain. The skin must look like a high-end beauty retouch that still retains a sense of living tissue: soft, even gradients with barely perceptible tonal shifts that prevent a "wax mannequin" look. Think of the skin as a fine watercolor wash — uniform at first glance, but with gentle tonal breathing up close. Eliminate all individual pores, freckle-scale spots, and digital noise. The needle will add its own texture on stone; the file must be clean but not sterile. Skin must appear as though the surface was cleaned to a smooth, even base and all definition was added ONLY through tonal transitions (light-to-shadow gradients) — NOT through surface grain, noise, or pore texture overlaid on a rough base. Any residual grain gets amplified by the needle into permanent spots on stone. Skin should have a subtle 'silver luminosity' — semi-specular highlights on forehead, cheekbones and nose that suggest polished, living tissue rather than matte wax.

2. **Hair Rendering (CRITICAL for Impact):**
   Render hair with DEFINED INDIVIDUAL STRANDS. White highlight streaks on hair take ABSOLUTE PRIORITY over dark strands. For impact engraving, white = engraved, gray = already "dark" for the machine. Generate PROMINENT WHITE HIGHLIGHT STREAKS following the hair growth direction, and minimize uniformly gray areas. Dark strands should only appear where they contrast directly with the face. Hair must have clear SCULPTURAL VOLUME — it must look like a 3D mass with a distinct beginning, curve, and end. Avoid flat hair that lies against the skull or evenly-toned areas without highlight streaks — these will appear bald on the engraved stone.

3. **Contrast & Edge Definition:**
   Render clothing and facial structure with macro-sharpness, ensuring every button, fabric weave, and fold is textured and clear. The impact machine "loves" when shadow is clearly separated from light — make transitions deliberate and bold, not gradual. Add a **rim light** along the subject's contour — as though a light source behind the subject catches the edges of the face, ears, hair, shoulders, and clothing. The rim light must appear ON the subject surface (illuminating skin, fabric edge, hair strands), NOT as a glow layer between subject and background. It must be UNEVEN — stronger on the illuminated side of the face, weaker or absent on the shadow side. It should look like a natural backlight reflex from the main light source — NOT like an artificial uniform halo.

**Goal (Impact Optimization):** Create a high-contrast memorial portrait optimized for diamond needle impact engraving. Target MID-KEY with enhanced volume — NOT High Key. The face must read through light-shadow contrast (Δ≥40), NOT through overall brightness.

Skin brightness 190-215 applies ONLY to highlight peaks (forehead, cheekbones, nose bridge) — these must be the BRIGHTEST spots. Shadow zones of the face (under-eyes, jawline, sides of nose) must remain at 130-170 — noticeably darker than highlights, roughly 1.5-2 stops below, to preserve sculptural volume. Deep eye socket shadows (5-40) are REQUIRED and give sculptural volume on stone — do NOT fill them with fill-light. Do NOT render the entire face at a uniform high brightness — the face must have clear directional lighting with distinct light and shadow sides. If raising brightness kills contrast — do NOT raise brightness. CONTRAST > BRIGHTNESS.

Bold highlight/shadow transitions, sculptural hair with DOMINANT white highlights, macro-sharp clothing details, and vibrant catchlights in the eyes to prevent a "plastic" appearance on the engraved surface. No skin area may exceed brightness 245 except eye whites (which may reach 250-255).
