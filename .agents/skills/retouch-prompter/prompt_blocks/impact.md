# Ударная гравировка (Impact Style)

1. **Face & Skin (Smooth Tonal Variation):**
   Render the skin smooth with subtle tonal micro-variation — even gradients with gentle tonal breathing. The skin must have sculptural volumetric depth through soft, even gradients with perceptible tonal shifts. Think of the skin as a fine watercolor wash — uniform at first glance, but with gentle tonal breathing up close. Eliminate all individual pores, freckle-scale spots, and digital noise. The needle will add its own texture on stone; the file must be clean but not sterile. Skin must appear as though the surface was cleaned to a smooth, even base with all definition rendered through tonal transitions (light-to-shadow gradients). Any residual grain gets amplified by the needle into permanent spots on stone. Skin should have a subtle 'silver luminosity' — semi-specular highlights on forehead, cheekbones and nose that suggest polished, living tissue.

2. **Hair Rendering (CRITICAL for Impact):**
   Render hair with DEFINED INDIVIDUAL STRANDS. White highlight streaks on hair take ABSOLUTE PRIORITY over dark strands. For impact engraving, white = engraved, gray = already "dark" for the machine. Generate PROMINENT WHITE HIGHLIGHT STREAKS following the hair growth direction, and minimize uniformly gray areas. Render dark strands as contrast elements against the face — direct tonal opposition. Hair must have clear SCULPTURAL VOLUME — it must look like a 3D mass with a distinct beginning, curve, and end. Render each lock with distinct volume and highlight streaks — flat, evenly-toned areas appear bald on engraved stone.

3. **Contrast & Edge Definition:**
   Render clothing and facial structure with macro-sharpness, ensuring every button, fabric weave, and fold is textured and clear. The impact machine "loves" when shadow is clearly separated from light — make transitions deliberate and bold, not gradual. Render dark clothing with visible fold highlights and fabric texture in the 60-90 range. Keep the full tonal range of fabric folds readable. Add a **rim light** along the subject's contour — as though a light source behind the subject catches the edges of the face, ears, hair, shoulders, and clothing. Apply rim lighting tracing the silhouette on the subject's surface (illuminating skin, fabric edges, hair strands). Vary rim light intensity — stronger on the illuminated side, weaker on the shadow side. Render it as a natural backlight reflex from the main light source.
   * **Clothing Edge Brightness:** Set the rim light on clothing edges above the adjacent fabric tone — readable on stone while remaining visually connected to the fabric. Keep the combined rim light and fabric brightness below 245.

**Goal (Impact Optimization):** Create a high-contrast memorial portrait optimized for diamond needle impact engraving. Target MID-KEY with enhanced volume. Define the face through light-shadow contrast (Δ≥40) — shape comes from tonal range between lit and shadow areas.

Skin brightness 190-215 applies ONLY to highlight peaks (forehead, cheekbones, nose bridge) — these must be the BRIGHTEST spots. Shadow zones of the face (under-eyes, jawline, sides of nose) must remain at 130-170 — noticeably darker than highlights, roughly 1.5-2 stops below, to preserve sculptural volume. Deep eye socket shadows (5-40) provide sculptural volume on stone — preserve them. Render the face with clear directional lighting and distinct light and shadow regions. Prioritize contrast over brightness.

Bold highlight/shadow transitions, sculptural hair with DOMINANT white highlights, macro-sharp clothing details, and vibrant catchlights in the eyes for sculptural clarity on the engraved surface. Dark clothing shadow floor: 15-20 minimum except deep seams. Maintain visible tonal variation across all fabric areas. No skin area may exceed brightness 245 except eye whites (which may reach 250-255).

## Calibration Note

- Face brightness: 170-215 (etalon P25=168, P95=217)
- Shadow floor: 2 (eye sockets 2-5)
- White ceiling: 245 (max face=244)
- Stone gamma: 0.88
