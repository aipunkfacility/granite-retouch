# Impact Style

Render skin clean of pores and noise, but with prominent sculptural form — the face must not look flat or porcelain. All zones of the face receive comparable light — no single zone becomes a bright patch. The face reads as medium gray overall with clearly distinct light and shadow zones. Light and shadow zones are clearly distinguishable, connected by gradual tonal transitions.

Forehead wrinkles are bold sculptural lines with strong tonal contrast — the shadow in the recess is significantly darker than the surrounding skin, and the raised edge beside it is distinctly lighter. Wrinkle shadows are deep and dark enough to read clearly against the mid-gray skin — faint tonal suggestions are unacceptable for impact engraving. Eye wrinkles and nasolabial folds are clearly visible as soft tonal lines — not erased, not exaggerated into deep grooves, but present and natural. The model draws wrinkles that may not be visible on the original photo — this is expected and desired for impact engraving.

Hair matches the source photo in length and style. Render it as a sculptural mass with prominent white highlight streaks — on stone, even mid-gray reads dark, so highlights take priority. Highlights are the dominant feature, flat mid-gray areas are not acceptable. Dark strands serve as contrast elements against the highlights.

Render clothing with pronounced three-dimensional fabric folds — each fold has a lit side, a shadow side, and a gradual tonal gradient between them. The fabric surface is completely smooth — no visible weave, no texture grain, no fabric micro-structure, no surface noise. Only gradual tonal gradients describe the folds and form. The clothing must look like a real photograph of real fabric, not a simplified painted version. Even the darkest zones retain tonal variation from fold structure — the clothing reads as dimensional fabric with depth, not as a flat dark area.
