# Impact Style

Render the skin smooth with visible tonal range — light and shadow zones clearly distinguishable, connected by smooth mid-tone gradients. Think of the skin as a sculpted form under directional light. Keep skin clean of pores, noise, and grain, but do not flatten the tonal range — the transitions between light and shadow must be visible and natural.

Hair matches the source photo in length and style. Render it as a sculptural mass with prominent white highlight streaks — on stone, even mid-gray reads dark, so highlights take priority. Minimize flat, uniformly gray areas. Dark strands serve as contrast elements.

Render clothing and facial structure with macro-sharpness. Make transitions deliberate and bold between shadow and light. Dark clothing retains visible tonal transitions throughout.
