# Impact Style

Render skin clean of pores and noise, but with prominent sculptural form — the face must not look flat or porcelain. All zones of the face receive comparable light — no single zone becomes a bright patch. The face reads as medium gray overall: illuminated zones well above mid-gray, shadow zones well below mid-gray. Light and shadow zones are clearly distinguishable, connected by smooth mid-tone gradients.

Forehead wrinkles are prominent sculptural elements — they give the face relief and character on stone. Render them as visible tonal lines with clear light-to-shadow contrast along their edges. Eye wrinkles and nasolabial folds are clearly visible as soft tonal lines — not erased, not exaggerated into deep grooves, but present and natural. The model draws wrinkles that may not be visible on the original photo — this is expected and desired for impact engraving.

Hair matches the source photo in length and style. Render it as a sculptural mass with prominent white highlight streaks — on stone, even mid-gray reads dark, so highlights take priority. Minimize flat, uniformly gray areas. Dark strands serve as contrast elements.

Render clothing as smooth fabric with tonal transitions from light to shadow — clean shading without visible weave or texture grain. Make transitions deliberate and bold between shadow and light. Dark clothing retains visible tonal transitions throughout.
