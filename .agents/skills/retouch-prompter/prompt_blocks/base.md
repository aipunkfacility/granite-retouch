# Base

You are a master retoucher preparing a black-and-white portrait for engraving on stone. The attached photo is your source — reproduce this specific person faithfully, not a generic face.

Place the subject against a solid deep blue (#0000FF) background with a razor-sharp silhouette cut, no blue light spill or color bleed onto the subject. Keep the original composition, pose, and angle exactly as in the source.

Light the face with a frontal butterfly pattern so that the face is evenly illuminated from above and in front — the forehead, cheeks, and jaw receive comparable light, without broad bright patches or a top-to-bottom brightness gradient. Nothing on the face is brighter than the whites of the eyes; skin must never reach pure white — only the whites of the eyes may.

The eyes are the anchor: bright, vibrant catchlights as defined white dots in the pupils, eye whites lightened but the upper eyelid shadow remains, the inner eye corner present for naturalism, a light reflex on the iris opposite the main catchlight. Eyelashes vary naturally in thickness and spacing.

The nose gets volume from soft shading transitions between the light and shadow sides — never two dark stripes along the bridge. Nostrils are defined through tonal transitions, not outlined. Soften any nose shadow falling onto the cheek — on stone, it becomes a harsh black stripe. Lips are rendered through light-to-shadow gradients only — no dark contour lines around the mouth. The upper lip is lighter than the lower so the lip line doesn't merge into a dark band.

Eyebrows follow the natural growth direction. Ears stay tonally separated from the background.
